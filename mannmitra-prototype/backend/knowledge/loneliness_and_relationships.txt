Feeling lonely in college:
Loneliness in a new or busy environment is common, even for students who are surrounded by people. It does not mean something is wrong with a person; it often reflects that meaningful connection takes time to build. Small, low-pressure steps, like joining one club activity or messaging a single classmate, tend to feel more achievable than trying to build a whole friend group at once.

Homesickness:
Missing home while adjusting to college life is a normal and common experience, not a sign of weakness. Keeping a regular, light-touch connection with family or friends from home, alongside slowly building new routines and connections at college, tends to ease the adjustment over time.

Friendship difficulties:
A disagreement or distance with a friend can feel especially heavy in a close-knit college environment. It often helps to separate the specific incident from the whole friendship, and to consider whether a direct, calm conversation with the person might clear up a misunderstanding, rather than assuming the worst.

Family pressure:
Pressure from family around academics, career choices, or expectations is common among college students and can be a significant source of stress. It can help to identify which specific expectation feels heaviest right now, rather than carrying a vague sense of overall pressure, since a specific concern is usually easier to think through or talk about.
