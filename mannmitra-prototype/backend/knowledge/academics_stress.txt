Managing exam pressure:
Feeling overwhelmed before exams is a common experience, not a sign of failure. Breaking the syllabus into smaller, specific sections and tackling one at a time, rather than trying to review everything at once, tends to reduce the feeling of being overwhelmed. Short, focused study blocks (around 25 minutes) with brief breaks in between are generally easier to sustain than long unbroken sessions.

Dealing with a heavy workload:
When several deadlines or exams land close together, it can help to write them all down and identify just the one that is closest, rather than trying to mentally juggle everything at once. Addressing one task fully before moving to the next often feels more manageable than switching between several partially-done tasks.

When concentration feels difficult:
Difficulty focusing is often connected to stress, tiredness, or an overwhelming task feeling too large to start. Starting with a very small, low-pressure version of the task (such as committing to just five minutes of it) can make it easier to begin, after which continuing often feels less difficult than starting did.

Coping with fear of failing:
Worry about failing an exam is a normal response to something that matters to a person, not evidence that failure is likely. Naming the specific worry out loud or in writing, and separating what is within a student's control (like how they prepare) from what is not (like the exact questions asked), can make the pressure feel more manageable.
